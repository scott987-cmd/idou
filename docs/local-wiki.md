# Local automatic knowledge node — development slice

Reading a complete Feishu docx/wiki document in a work task emits an accepted-read event. The main process asynchronously builds a source-evidence page with lossless section-sized text chunks (relative to the reader's normalized text), source URL, resource identity, revision, content hash, tenant, principal, and successful-read ACL evidence. Repeated reads update the same principal-scoped page. Optional automatic model synthesis now adds cited facts to those pages. This remains a development knowledge node, **not an ontology or a deployed distributed enterprise knowledge base**.

The **企业知识库** entry searches those pages and offers **在工作任务中打开**. Opening a result reuses the authoritative document reader and its side-by-side Agent references. By default no model is called by reading, indexing or searching; after explicitly enabling automatic synthesis, subsequent accepted reads may trigger the bounded model workflow below. Search itself does not initiate a model call. No new copy is published to Feishu or stored by the control server.

Selected Feishu conversations can now feed this same pipeline through a native background document-discovery worker. One confirmed scope discovers and reads supported links automatically, without opening each document or creating tasks; chat bodies do not become Wiki pages. See [background discovery, cancellation and explicit limits](message-discovery.md).

The native [reading-driven publication worker](wiki-reading-publication.md) now derives eligible sources from these accepted local reads and obtains a new server scope for a changed set. `localReadAt` is additive encrypted metadata, not an ACL: legacy and import-only pages cannot silently become automatic publication sources. Full source/key checks still run before publishing. The production desktop has not enabled this worker or bypassed its CLI identity gate.

The native [portable bundle import](wiki-bundles.md) can also bring in another node's cited synthesis after fresh recipient reads of every source, exact version/text checks and one guarded encrypted commit. It reconstructs recipient ownership/ACL evidence and marks imported synthesis as a publisher claim; it does not call a model. Normal search and opening the result retain their source authorization checks. This path is currently exercised by a desktop fixture, not an enabled production sync UI: trusted key authorization, automatic publishing and retrieval remain unfinished.

## What a larger copy costs

Measured outside Electron, one process per size, with an instant provider so the numbers are local work only. "First" is once per application start; "after" is what every question pays.

| documents | text | first build | every search after | heap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 200 | 1.3 M chars | 0.3 s | ~25 ms | 50 MB |
| 1,000 | 6.5 M chars | 1.4 s, or 0.15 s from the saved profiles | ~40 ms | 180 MB |
| 10,000 | 65 M chars | 14 s, or 1.1 s from the saved profiles | ~20 ms | 660 MB |

The question's rarest words decide which passage is quoted. BM25 sums over every term of a question, and a question is mostly common words: asked "我是 P6，下周去南京出差，酒店一晚最多能报多少", the store answered from three FAQ paragraphs that repeat 出差/住宿/报销 and hold no figure, while the table carrying 「P6」and「南京」sat eleventh. Each passage is now lifted by how much of the question's *rarity* it carries — the rarest quarter of the terms the store actually holds, weighted by how rare each is, so that 我/是/下/周 cannot stand in for a name or a place, and a word that appears nowhere takes no place at all. Measured: full evidence **71% → 75%** on the evaluation corpus and **68% → 75%** with a thousand unrelated documents around it, with nothing lost (every question still receives some of its evidence) and the near-duplicate case unchanged at 50%.

What did not work is worth recording, because each sounded right: capping how many excerpts one document may contribute (57–71%, worse), re-verifying more documents per question (71% at 8, 10 and 12; 68% at 16), ranking more passages per document (71% at every depth from 10 to 80), quoting a whole small table instead of the matched rows (71%, unchanged), and feeding the first pass's vocabulary back into a second query (75%, but trading two questions for two others, and collapsing to 61% when fed more). The budget was full in every one of those runs: what decides an answer is which passages fill it, not how many are allowed to.

A second round went after the questions that need a person's row from a roster, and failed for a reason worth keeping: a floor guaranteeing that every distinctive word of a question is quoted by some passage (68%, because the reserved room cost more than it bought), then the same floor requiring a *second* document to speak about that word (75%, unchanged).

A third round built the entity index those questions actually call for, and it is the most useful negative result of the three. Table rows are records: a row's subject is its first name-like cell, a row needs three columns to be a record at all (「时间｜2026-07-08」is a property, not a person), and the row whose *first* cell is the name is that name's definition rather than a mention of it. That extraction is good — 203 subjects on the evaluation corpus, people and departments and companies, with 杭州 and 部门经理 correctly keying nothing, built in 26 ms over a thousand documents. Wired into a search it does exactly what it promises: asked who must approve 韩啸's purchase, the answer now carries 「韩啸｜平台研发部｜部门经理｜M2」 from the roster, which no ranking had ever reached.

It still was not worth shipping, and the reason is the same constraint underneath all three rounds: **the 12,000-character budget is always full**. A record row can enter only by displacing a passage, and measured over both corpora what it displaces is worth more than what it adds — with a 300-character reserve, full evidence stayed at 75% and one question (q15) lost *all* of its evidence at a thousand documents; with no reserve, the rows never fit and the mechanism is inert. Passage-level delivery moved 78% → 79% on the clean corpus and not at all at scale.

The conclusion is not "entities do not help". It is that a second fact belongs in a second question, not in the same prompt: the agent already has `kb-search` and `kb-read`, and a lookup it makes itself gets its own budget instead of competing with the evidence for the first half of the question. That is where this should be tried next, and the extraction rules above are written down so it need not be rediscovered.

Retrieval is two stages, which is what makes that possible. Choosing which documents deserve a Feishu round-trip is a document-level question, answered from a **profile**: the ~800 terms that distinguish that document from the rest of this person's store, interned to ids and held as typed arrays (`src/knowledge/retrieval.js`). Passage-level BM25 then runs only on the handful of documents that come back verified, where it decides which passage to quote. Keeping every chunk of every document indexed in memory instead cost a gigabyte at ten thousand documents and was rebuilt to answer one question.

Everything expensive is cached by **content**, never by object identity, because verification rebuilds every page it re-reads and a restart rebuilds all of them: term counts under the text, the document index and the standing graph under an order-independent signature of (owner, title, content hash). The store re-sorts itself by what was used most recently, so an order-sensitive signature missed on every search. A cached index holds the pages it was built from, so ranked hits are mapped back to the live pages before anything acts on them.

Two limits are worth knowing before growing a store past a few thousand documents:

- **The first build is paid once, not once per start.** Tokenising every document costs about 1.3 s at a thousand documents and 14 s at ten thousand. Those profiles are now written next to the store as `local-wiki.enc.profiles` — gzipped, encrypted with the same key, `0600` — and read back on load: 0.15 s and 1.1 s for the same two stores, measured end to end through `LocalWiki`. The file holds each document's own distinguishing terms and nothing else; a document that is removed, or whose text changed, has its terms rewritten off the disk at once rather than at the next throttled write, and when the last document goes the file goes. Anything unreadable — another machine's key, a truncated write, a term list for text that has since changed — is discarded silently and the store simply pays the slow first question. Coverage is unaffected: the scaled evaluation scores 68% / 100% / 100% either way, missing exactly the same questions.

That last claim turned out to be incomplete, and the way it failed is worth keeping. Profiles alone are not enough to restore a ranking: which documents are worth re-reading also depends on document frequencies — in how many documents each term appears — and those were recomputed on every build from whatever was at hand, full text for a document not seen before but only the 800 profile terms for one whose profile was cached. So the same store ranked differently after a restart. The scaled evaluation happened not to lose a gold document over it, but on the real account the question "生产临时权限最多开多久" stopped choosing the report that announced the change to 8 hours, and the model answered with the superseded 24. Measured on the evaluation corpus, 32 of 32 questions had a different top eight after a restart. Frequencies are now counted over whole documents in every path, stored in the same sidecar with the exact set of documents they describe, reused when a build covers exactly those documents, adjusted document by document when up to 64 have changed, and recounted in full otherwise. The same probe now finds 0 of 32 different at 16 and at 1,016 documents; a restart's first question at a thousand documents costs about 315 ms against 1.45 s cold (the frequencies add ~40 ms to loading), and `test/knowledge-frequency.test.js` pins both the identical ranking across a restart and incremental counts equal to a recount.
- **Near-duplicates defeat retrieval, and volume does not.** With a thousand unrelated documents added around the evaluation corpus, coverage is unchanged (68% full evidence, the right document present every time). With near-duplicate copies of the same policies — the same text with different numbers — coverage collapsed to 11%, with the right document found 14% of the time, because eight copies of one policy fill the prompt and nothing in their text says which one governs. Grouping them (below) takes the same corpus back to 50% / 57%; the unrelated-noise case is unchanged. That is a recovery, not a fix: what a store full of silent copies can honestly give is one copy plus the fact that the others exist.

## How the copy sits on disk

`local-wiki.enc` used to be one encrypted file holding every page, so reading one new document rewrote all of it: **18.5 MiB and 34 ms to record one arrival** at a thousand documents, growing with the store. It is now one encrypted file per document under `local-wiki.enc.d/`, named by a digest of the document's owner, plus a manifest that keeps the original path and says which files count right now (`src/knowledge/page-store.js`). Writing one document costs **146 KiB and 3–4 ms** — the page plus the manifest — and the manifest deliberately holds no text: an owner, a size, and a 16-hex digest of the state each page was written at, which is how a save knows what it does not have to write again.

What the single file bought is kept. A change still becomes visible in one rename — the manifest — so an interrupted save leaves the previous set intact rather than half of the next one, and the lease check a bundle import makes right before committing still guards that same moment. Removal still takes the bytes off this disk: the page file is unlinked when the manifest stops naming it, rather than lingering inside a blob nobody rewrites until the next change.

Three costs are the honest other side. Writing a whole store at once — a migration, or a bundle of many sources — went from 40 ms to about 220 ms at a thousand documents, because it creates a thousand files instead of one. Loading is unchanged (≈140 ms). And a page file that cannot be read now costs one document rather than the whole copy: it is skipped, counted, and said out loud in 企业知识库's status line, where before a single unreadable byte refused the entire store.

A machine holding the old single-file copy migrates on first load: the pages are written back one file each and the old blob at that path becomes the manifest. Nothing is re-read from Feishu, and `test/knowledge-page-store.test.js` pins the migration, the orphan sweep after an interrupted save, the damaged-page case and the removal.

## Seeing and managing what is stored

企业知识库 lists every source this login holds — kind, self-declared date, standing, near-duplicate count, size, when the person read it and when it was last used — with no network call at all (a listing is not an answer, so nothing is re-verified for it) and no excerpt text. Each row can be removed: that takes this machine's copy only, the Feishu document is untouched, and reading it again rebuilds the copy. A source can also be added on purpose by pasting a link, instead of only as a side effect of having opened one in a task.

Nothing leaves the copy silently any more. A source can go without the person removing it in five ways — unused for the retention period, pushed out by the document ceiling, pushed out by the byte ceiling, refused by Feishu three reads in a row, or its link now reading as a different document or account — and each departure is recorded with its title, link, reason and time, committed by the same manifest rename that drops the page, and shown at the top of 企业知识库 (even when the list is empty, which is exactly when it matters) and in the status line. What the person removes themselves is not recorded: they know. The record is scoped to the account it belongs to, survives a restart, and keeps the last twenty departures for as long as a document itself would have been kept. Pinned by `test/knowledge-gone.test.js` and by `smoke-wiki-bundle-desktop.js`, which drives three refused reads in Electron and asserts the notice.

## Which version is in force

`src/knowledge/standing.js` decides, by arithmetic over the documents' own words, whether a stored document has been repealed or partly amended by another one this person holds. Three rules bound it, each forced by a measurement on the evaluation corpus:

- **It only ever demotes** (`prior <= 1`, superseded documents 0.75). A quarter of the evaluation questions have the gold passage within 7% of the strongest distractor and a tenth have it behind, so a prior wide enough to promote would flip right and wrong answers at random.
- **It only demotes what another document says, in words, is repealed** — «《X》…已废止» with the citation and the verb in one clause, outside tables (a comparison table listing "已废止" is describing, not declaring), and only when the repealing document is not older than its victim. The sentence travels with the judgement everywhere it is shown or sent.
- **The budget is spent on what is in force first.** The excerpt limit is what a question can carry, and ranking alone let repealed documents take the room their replacement needed: with a two-excerpt budget, a store holding two short archived policies and one long current handbook sent *only the repealed ones*, so the answer would have quoted the old figure. In-force documents now fill the budget before any repealed one is considered; the repealed text still follows, capped at two excerpts each, because it is the only thing that can answer "what changed". Measured on the corpora: unchanged (71% on the fixture, 68% / 100% / 100% at a thousand documents), which is the point — this is a floor, not a ranking change. Pinned by `test/knowledge-standing.test.js`.
- **A sentence that names several documents repeals all of them.** «《A》《B》予以废止» used to demote only B, because the reader kept the last citation before the verb. Citations chained to the verb by list punctuation (、，和与及以及, or nothing at all) now all count, while a citation separated by a verb or another clause — «按《A》执行，《B》予以废止» — still does not.
- **Everything else is a label**: `amended` (a later decision changed part of it), `conflict` (two documents disagree about which is current), the document's self-declared date and its kind. A repealed document is still sent — at most two excerpts of it — because it is the only thing that can answer "what changed".

On the 16-document corpus this produces exactly one repealed document and four amendment edges, with no false positives; the same fixture pins it (`test/knowledge-standing.test.js`). The person sees a badge, the self-declared date, and a banner naming the successor and quoting the sentence, with the document still shown in full underneath. The model receives the same fields plus one paragraph telling it never to state a repealed rule as current, to answer from the named successor, and to say which version it used.

## When the same document is stored twice

`src/knowledge/duplicates.js` notices that stored documents are near-copies of each other, sends one of them, and says the others exist. It never decides which copy is correct: where a document says in words that it replaces another, the section above reads that and the answer follows it; where nothing says anything, this is an ambiguity to report.

Each document is reduced to a bottom-k sketch (the 64 smallest hashes of its terms), and only documents that agree on a whole band of that sketch are compared, so a store of unique documents costs one pass and no comparisons. Measured on the 16-document corpus: unrelated documents peak at 0.34 — including the 2025 and 2026 travel policies, which are versions of each other but genuinely different texts — while a copy with changed numbers sits at 0.98. The threshold is 0.65, with a wide margin on both sides, and `test/knowledge-duplicates.test.js` pins both ends against the fixture corpus.

Which copy stands for the group is deliberately explainable and never a judgement about correctness: a copy whose last check failed goes last — the rest of the group is held back for the representative, so an unreadable one would silence all of them, while a repealed one still arrives and is labelled — then a document another document declares repealed, then the later self-declared date, then the one this person actually opened, then the fuller text, then the id so the same store always answers the same way. The person sees the copy count on every row of 企业知识库 and which copy is being sent, so the extras can be removed; the answer carries a `duplicates` field naming the others, and one paragraph telling the model to say that several copies exist and to flag any number that could differ between them.

Two things then had to be fixed before grouping actually paid, both found by measuring where the right document went:

- **An archive must not speak for the original.** Between two identical copies with no date and nobody having opened either — a store filled by pasting links or by automatic discovery — the choice came down to their ids, a coin flip that lost the current document in 7 of 16 cases. A title that says 归档、存档、旧版、历史版本、备份、副本、作废、过期 now goes last, above only the unreadable and the repealed. It is the same principle as standing: read what the document says about itself.
- **Copies must not flatten the store's statistics.** Which documents are worth re-reading was decided from term frequencies over *every* stored page, so thirteen archived copies of a roster made every term in it look ordinary — it appeared in fourteen "documents" — and the roster stopped ranking for the questions only it can answer. Those frequencies are now computed over the documents that can actually be chosen, one per group.

Measured on the evaluation corpus with ~200 near-duplicate distractors: full evidence 11% → 50% → **71%**, right document present 14% → 57% → **96%**, all needed documents present 32% → **86%**. The clean corpus and the thousand-unrelated-document case are unchanged, and `test/knowledge-eval.test.js` now runs the corpus a second time with three archived copies of every document, with floors under both.

## Where an answer says it came from

An answer's sources are shown under it as cards, one per document: what it is, the date it gives itself, its standing badge, and how many near-identical copies the store holds. The documents the answer actually cites — its `sourceUrl`, its short id, or its exact title appearing in the text — are marked 已引用 and come first; the rest are still listed, because a model that used a document without citing it is exactly the case a person needs to see. The line above them also says how many documents could not be re-verified this turn and so took no part in the answer. Clicking a card opens that document in the task, through the same authorized read as anywhere else.

The cards are built from the evidence that was sent (`knowledgeSources` in `src/knowledge/task-scope.js`), recorded on the question they were sent with, and bounded — at most twelve documents, three section names each, and no excerpt text, because the message keeps them for as long as the task exists.

The same record lets the answer's own links be checked. A model copying a Feishu address into its reply sometimes gets it slightly wrong — in two live evaluation rounds a domain lost its last three letters once, and a 27-character document token came back with one character changed the next time — so the link opened nothing while the answer around it was right. When a reply is rendered, each link is compared with the documents sent for that question (`src/desktop/renderer/source-links.js`): an exact address is left alone; one that is the same kind of document, on the same host or a few characters off it, with a token identical or within two characters of exactly one source (and every other source at least five away), is pointed at that source's real address, with 已更正 beside it; anything else is left exactly as written, because sending a link to a different legitimate document would be worse than leaving it broken. The answer's text is never changed, and a slightly wrong address still counts as citing its source for the 已引用 mark.

## What can be a source

Three kinds, each read and re-verified through its own reader, dispatched by link shape (`src/knowledge/sheet-source.js`):

- **Documents** (docx/wiki) enter as before, on an accepted full read in a task.
- **Spreadsheets** enter as one page per worksheet, read whole: page by page (each page within the API's 200 rows and 2,000 cells) under one revision, which every page is checked against and which is read once more at the end, so the copy is one version of the sheet rather than a walk across edits. The bounds are the store's — 5,000 rows, 100,000 cells or 450,000 characters of projection, whichever binds first, because an evidence page may not exceed half a million characters — and a sheet beyond them keeps its first rows and says how many, in the text the model reads and in the page's warnings. Formulas, styles and charts are not copied. Before this a sheet was stored as its first 200 rows, so a question about row 2,734 of a ledger could not be answered, or was answered from rows that happened to be there.
- **Re-verifying a sheet does not re-read it.** Every question re-checks its sources, and paging through five thousand rows for each question would make a large table unaffordable to ask about. A spreadsheet has its own revision: asked as the current user, it proves both that the person can still open the sheet and that the stored cells are what Feishu holds, and only when it has moved is the sheet read again. A refused revision call is treated exactly like a refused read — the page is marked unverified and nothing stored is quoted. A revision cannot vouch for two things. A copy stored before whole-table reads was cut at 200 rows, and its revision may never have moved; so every copy now opens with a note naming the bounds it was taken under (「表格值快照：本机副本每张表最多整理 5000 行、100000 个单元格、450000 字…」), a copy without the note for today's bounds is read in full once, and because the bounds are hashed into the copy the replacement is written to disk and a restart does not repeat it. A copy is also read in full once when anything after that note is not a sentence a whole-table read writes today: earlier builds kept Feishu's `warning_message` there — advice to the program reading the sheet, not a finding about it (see [the spreadsheet reader](feishu-sheet-reader.md)) — and every hit showed it as though something were wrong with the sheet. And a value a formula recomputes without anyone editing the sheet — `TODAY()`, a reference to another workbook — may move no revision, so it is only as fresh as the sheet's last edit.
- **A question naming an identifier gets the row that holds it.** Two things stood in the way. A 3,000-row ledger holds 3,000 contract numbers and a document's profile keeps its 800 most distinguishing terms, so 「合同 QL-HT-2026-2734 的到期日」 did not bring the ledger into the top eight at all; codes, order numbers and long numbers a question names are now looked for verbatim in the stored text, and up to two documents holding one are read as well. And reaching the table was not enough. Asked 「合同 QL-HT-2026-0198 的服务到期日和状态是什么？」, the real application ranked a 121-row ledger first, verified it, and sent its first ten rows: the row carries that one word of the question and nothing else, so passages repeating 合同、到期、状态 filled the 12,000-character budget first. A passage holding an identifier the question names is now quoted before anything the ranking prefers — at most two per identifier and four in all, matched regardless of letter case. Either way an identifier found in more than three documents — a date, usually — is ignored as not identifying anything. Beside the evaluation corpus, four such questions against a 121-row and a 3,000-row ledger sent the row 3 times in 8 before and 8 in 8 after; none of the 32 evaluation questions names an identifier, so their scores cannot move, and `test/knowledge-eval.test.js` now carries the ledger scenario.
- **Whole-table questions are answered by listing, not paging.** `kb-read --doc <id> --match <词>` returns every line of a document containing a word — for a table, every matching row with its header — up to 80 rows and 12,000 characters, and says how many matched when it cannot list them all; the note attached to a table excerpt tells the model to use it before counting or filtering.
- **Base tables** enter as one page per table, projected from the field names and the records, through the read-only API passthrough (`src/providers/feishu/base-reader.js`, scopes `base:app:read` / `base:table:read` / `base:record:read`). Records are read page by page, 500 per request, up to 2,000 records or 450,000 characters; before this only the first page was kept. A Base has no document revision, so the projection's own digest is its revision: any change to the records this copy holds replaces the page — and with nothing cheaper to ask, a question that needs the table reads it again, at most four pages of records after the app, table and field listings. Attachments, formula results and view filters are not expanded, and the projection says which other tables the same Base holds.

A person adds a source on purpose from 企业知识库 by pasting a link (`add-knowledge`), and a spreadsheet opened in a task is kept the same way a document read is. Everything else is unchanged: the same identity and permission re-check before anything is stored or returned, the same encrypted local file, the same retention.

A document's **embedded** spreadsheets and Base tables are not copied into its text — the reader leaves a placeholder — so the page keeps their links instead (`embeds`), and the excerpts sent to a model carry them. An answer that needs a number living inside an embedded sheet can then say where it is, or the person can add that sheet as its own source.

## Automatic cited synthesis

Use **本次会话开启自动归纳** once; subsequent full-document reads automatically attempt synthesis for new retained source versions. The UI explains the selected gateway, text transfer and possible cost. Enabling verifies the existing Feishu tenant/principal, gateway session and OS encryption. This temporary authorization is held in memory, bound to that principal, the exact gateway token/origin and the model the server enforces, and expires with the development session. Changing the connection disables it; a mismatched source identity cannot use it. It is off again after restart. This client consent is **not** enterprise administrator policy or proof that the development gateway identity is Feishu-enrolled.

The client sends only a title and bounded text chunks through the existing authenticated `/v1/responses` gateway, not a provider key or the source's tenant/principal metadata. The request names the chat model the server enforces — MiniMax-M3 through the [MiniMax native Responses contract](https://platform.minimaxi.com/docs/api-reference/responses-create) by default, or GLM-5.3 through a loopback LiteLLM proxy when the server sets `IDOU_MODEL_PROVIDER=litellm` — with nonstreaming output, no tools, `store: false`, and that model's output budget (`sideCalls` in `src/providers/codex/chat-models.js`): at most 2400 output tokens on MiniMax-M3 and 8192 on GLM-5.3, whose reasoning counts against the cap. The desktop learns that model from the server's health endpoint; while the server has named none this build ships, synthesis cannot be enabled and no text is sent, rather than assuming MiniMax-M3. Each saved synthesis keeps the model that produced it. At most 12000 UTF-16 text units from complete leading chunks are included; the UI reports included/total chunk coverage. Long-source map/reduce and cross-document synthesis are not yet implemented.

Each result contains 1–6 model-authored points with 1–3 exact source quotes each. Quote chunk IDs must belong to the actual sent input; quotes must occur contiguously in those chunks. The application reconstructs their offsets itself and revalidates saved facts on restoration. Unknown chunks, fabricated quotes, malformed/oversized JSON, tool outputs, incomplete responses, model mismatch, cancellation and timeout fail closed. An incomplete response has already been billed, which is why GLM's budget is larger: its reasoning must not use up the cap before the JSON begins. **Matching a quote proves traceability, not that the model's inference is true or entailed by that quote.** The interface exposes the quotes and labels that limitation.

Before dispatch, the source is read again and must still match the observed tenant/principal, resource, revision and content. A durable encrypted `reserved` marker is committed before the paid call. The source is checked again before accepting the output. Source changes/revocation discard the result. Queries expose synthesis only after their normal current-source validation, and a changed revision loses the old synthesis.

The development ceiling is six attempted model calls per application startup, not a billing or enterprise quota service. Turning the feature off/on does not reset this counter. The same **retained** source/recipe version is not automatically retried after failure, ambiguous completion, interruption or restart; the reservation survives inside its encrypted page. Eviction/removal of that page also removes its reservation, so this is not a permanent cross-device billing ledger. One refusal is different: a gateway enforcing another model answers `model_not_allowed` before any upstream call, so nothing was billed. The refused page's reservation and its call are given back rather than kept `failed`, automatic synthesis is turned off because every later page would be refused the same way, and the page can be summarised once the person enables synthesis again for the model the server now enforces. **关闭并取消归纳** aborts the active model request and invalidates late results; a request already sent may have incurred cost. The client gives up on this workflow's request after 90 seconds on MiniMax-M3 and 180 seconds on GLM-5.3, inside the gateway's own upstream timeouts (180 and 600 seconds). Serial indexing/query operations may wait behind the active synthesis; a separate worker/cancellable CLI queue remains future work.

An optional online login renewal may rebind an already-enabled, unexpired synthesis consent to the successor Agent session during the same application startup. Rotation waits for the current local Wiki operation, including a dispatched model request and its durable result/reservation. It then requires a fresh matching Feishu tenant/principal, the same model-gateway origin and model, a different session binding and a strictly later expiry. The local six-call counter is preserved. Explicit disable, expiry, changed identity/gateway/model, a failed login or an invalid successor leaves synthesis off and requires manual re-enablement; no failed/reserved source is replayed. This does not create restart persistence, administrator consent or an uninterrupted-stream guarantee.

## Identity and access

- Observations must carry a nonempty verified tenant key and principal. Missing tenant information pauses ingestion; neither a hostname nor a local model session substitutes for tenant identity. Successful live CLI identity-envelope compatibility remains unverified on this machine.
- Partial/anchored reads are excluded instead of silently fetching more than the user opened or overwriting a full page with an excerpt. Embedded resources remain explicitly unloaded according to reader warnings.
- Pages are scoped by tenant, provider, resource and observing principal. They are not shared between two users just because their source document IDs match.
- Query candidates are selected locally by BM25 over section-sized chunks (`src/knowledge/retrieval.js`: CJK characters and bigrams plus Latin words, document-frequency weighted), but no cached title, excerpt or source link reaches the renderer before a fresh provider read succeeds under the current tenant/principal. Excerpts are always built from the text that was just re-read, never from the stored copy the ranking ran against; revision changes replace old content. A redirected wiki link resolving to a different resource does not inherit access to the old page.
- A question may bring back several passages from one document — the rule and the table it refers to usually live apart — bounded at 20 rows and 12,000 characters in total. Each excerpt is an exact slice of the document, grown from the matched chunk to the heading above it, with the header row of a table it belongs to carried alongside as a label. `test/knowledge-eval.test.js` holds the floor: 16 generated documents, 32 questions, and the share whose answer actually reaches the prompt.
- A final identity check rejects the whole response if the account changes during verification. A failed candidate read — including a transient network error — no longer removes the page: it is kept, marked stale, and excluded from every answer, graph and publication until a read succeeds again; the third consecutive failure removes it. A read that returns a different resource behind the same link is removed at once. Neither case deletes the source document, and reading it again rebuilds the copy.
- Only the documents a query actually wants are re-read, up to eight per search, four at a time, instead of re-reading all ten candidates one after another. There is no "recently verified" cache: a second search in the same turn re-reads, because a window of trust is exactly how a link repointed at another document would slip through.
- These are point-in-time read checks. Already displayed results are not a live subscription to ACL changes. There is no offline cached-answer fallback and no authorization guarantee for other arbitrary Agent shell tools.

## Local persistence and limits

The application owns `knowledge/local-wiki.enc` inside its Electron user-data directory. It encrypts the whole versioned JSON snapshot, including titles, links, principal metadata and chunks, using [Electron safeStorage](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/safe-storage). Encryption must be available; Linux must report a recognized secret-store backend, never `basic_text` or `unknown`. There is no plaintext fallback or automatic Keychain reconfiguration. The development implementation uses the synchronous safeStorage API; its OS interaction and whole-snapshot cost need measurement on packaged target platforms.

Writes use a mode-0600 sibling temporary file and atomic rename within a mode-0700 application-created directory. The previous file remains untouched if loading/decryption/format validation fails. This is not a backup, cross-process locking protocol, fsync-based power-loss guarantee, or defense against an already compromised OS account. Operating-system encryption protections differ by platform. Task/Codex conversation history is a separate existing store and is **not** encrypted or redacted by this feature.

Current development defaults are a **10 MiB encrypted-file ceiling**, **200 pages**, and **30 days since the last observed read**. Recording/querying prunes expired or oldest derived pages; there is no wake-up timer while the app is idle or closed. These are local development bounds, not the administrator-configurable shared Drive budget. Automatic eviction never touches source documents or task files. An observation too large to retain reports that it was not kept.

The background queue accepts at most 32 operations. Shutdown refuses new observations, drains accepted work and clears the in-memory page collection. Querying is serialized with writes and verifies at most 10 locally ranked candidates. Matching is bounded lexical substring matching (including Chinese), not embeddings or semantic search. Queries can wait for multiple CLI reads/timeouts; cancellable parallel source verification remains future work. Changed documents not selected by the old local query may require another normal read before their new terms become discoverable.

## Verification and remaining scope

- Unit tests exercise the actual read-event consumer, encrypted disk bytes, file mode, restart restoration, exact Unicode chunk reconstruction/citations, deduplication, tenant/user isolation, revoked access, source revision/redirect changes, missing identity, unavailable encryption, corrupted snapshots, byte/count/retention limits, late older observations and shutdown.
- A controlled in-memory mutation in an isolated test process replaced query verification with returning cached content. The revocation test failed on the leaked original excerpt, confirming that the check detects the intended access-control regression. No production file was modified for this experiment.
- `npm run test:document-desktop` operates real Electron UI/IPC with synthetic CLI envelopes, a model fixture and a **test-only AES-GCM cipher**, not live Feishu, a live chat model or a real OS Keychain. It opens a document, automatically collects it, queries, opens the result as a task, restarts, refreshes from a different source version, enables automatic synthesis, expands original citations, disables synthesis and verifies revoked sources disappear. Production does not import the fixture bootstrap or cipher.
- `docs/evidence/desktop-knowledge-fixture.png` shows the actual rendered knowledge UI with explicitly synthetic source data. It was visually inspected, including inert display of a script-looking source string.
- The synthesis increment made one real MiniMax request using a 103-character synthetic project plan through the gateway. It returned five traceable points, with 840 total tokens reported. Every quote matched its source offsets; the repeated read made no second request. No real enterprise read/write, shared upload or secret-store configuration change was performed. The last live CLI attempt failed with an uninitialized Keychain before content fetch; it was not bypassed here.
- `scripts/smoke-wiki-synthesis.js --live` is an explicit paid smoke entry, separate from default tests. It reads the chat-model settings and key only through the server's own loader, so it exercises whichever model the deployment file selects (MiniMax-M3, or GLM-5.3 with `IDOU_MODEL_PROVIDER=litellm`), and checks that the saved synthesis names that model. It starts a temporary loopback gateway, verifies actual automatic synthesis/citations/deduplication using synthetic source and encryption adapters, then revokes its session, closes the server and removes its disposable test directory. It is not a real Feishu/Keychain or full live desktop test. The recorded live run above is MiniMax-M3. In the live GLM runs of 2026-09-11/12 the same smoke passed against GLM-5.3 through the real LiteLLM v1.96.0 (Volcengine Ark, model group `volc-agent`): one request, **1851 total tokens** for the same 103-character source that took 840 on MiniMax-M3, cited facts whose every quote matched its source offsets, and no second request on the repeated read. It ran under the 2400-token cap then in force; that gap on a tiny source is why GLM synthesis now gets 8192 tokens and 180 seconds. No live GLM run has yet summarised a full 12000-character page or run at the new budget.

Still required by the full design: real Feishu SSO/CLI identity linkage and private-provider compatibility; secure history policy; broader authorized activity ingestion; cross-document synthesis and evidence-backed entities/relations; local embeddings/hybrid retrieval; administrator controls; production key authorization and Drive generation publishing, quota reconciliation and revocation/tombstone propagation; automatic scheduling through the implemented distributed leases and live multi-client verification. This slice must not be presented as completion of those requirements.
